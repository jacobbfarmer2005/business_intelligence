# Midwest Airbnb Analysis
Data Caveats: Two caveats matter for your write-up: a night that is not available is either booked or blocked by the host (the data cannot tell which), and Inside Airbnb uses reviews as a stand-in for completed stays, so monthly reviews measure demand, not bookings. The 2026 calendar files carry no nightly prices, so the only price is the one in listings.

## Task 8: What the join lost.
**456** listings have no rows in `availability`. Chicago loses the most listings (267) compared to other cities when using a join.

## Findings & Overview:
1) Superhosts charge about 44 dollars more per night than other hosts for entire homes in Chicago and 41 dollars more in Columbus, but about 1 dollar less in the Twin Cities.
2) Hosts with ten or more listings hold 37 percent of Columbus's listings and 32 percent of Chicago's, but only 17 percent of the Twin Cities.
3) Only 19 percent of Columbus entire homes are mostly booked for October 2026, the lowest share of the three cities. Fall may be the most available time to visit.
4) The top 10 percent of listings earn 41 percent of estimated revenue in Chicago and 40 percent in the Twin Cities, compared with 36 percent in Columbus. The same listings earned much of the revenue in a city.
5) North Linden is the cheapest well-rated Columbus neighbourhood for groups, at a median of $32.60 per person per night for entire homes that sleep four or more.
